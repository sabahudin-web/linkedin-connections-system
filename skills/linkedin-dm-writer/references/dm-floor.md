# The DM floor: laws for every first LinkedIn message (step 10)

Adapted from the serve-before-sell DM method this system's author uses on his own leads (`acquisition-linkedin-dm-sell-by-chat`). If any other instruction conflicts with this file, this file wins. Read `voice.md` alongside it.

These leads are usually already 1st-degree connections, so "glad we connected" can be false weeks later. Open on their first name and go straight in.

## The laws

1. **Stranger test.** The reader decides in about 2 seconds. If it needs a setup paragraph, it is dead. Target shape: one short warm line, one line about their reality, one question. Nothing more.
2. **Open from their reality, never from a pitch.** Use the one true thing about them from the brief: what their business does (`About`, `Website Scrape`, `Company Notes`), the tension behind what they posted about (`Latest Post Topic`, `Hook`), what probably eats their week (`Pain Hypothesis`). Use it as the reason behind the question. Never quote their post, never write "saw your post", never state the pain hypothesis as a fact about them.
3. **Three parts, this order only.**
   - A short warm line with their first name ("Hey Lena,").
   - A self-aware line about their world that quietly shows you get it.
   - One easy question, answerable in about 3 words, with two easy doors ("the notes, or the follow-up email?"). Never a yes/no that dead-ends. Never "can we hop on a call?".
4. **Message 1 carries zero pitch.** No offer, no service name, no price, no link, no "I help X do Y", no "I build". The pitch only happens after they reply and say what they want fixed.
5. **Low-effort question.** About them, costs nothing to answer, lets them sound smart. Three words should be a complete reply.
6. **Under 300 characters,** spaces included. Trim the observation line to fit, never the question.
7. **Merge only the first name.** Never write a messy company name ("MMIO | RevOps & Inbound") into the message. If the data is not clean, stay universal.
8. **Sound like the sender.** Direct, casual, real. Short lines. No corporate polish, no em dashes. If anyone else could have sent it, it is not done.
9. **8th grade is the ceiling.** No word a 13-year-old would stop on, sentences under about 15 words, one idea then a full stop.
10. **Their language.** Write in the language in the `Language` column, typography included.

## Skip or hold before writing

- `SKIP: <reason>` in the cell for anyone the user's exclusions rule out (competitors, the wrong industry, outside their geography).
- `HOLD: <reason>` when `Notes & Cautions` says personal hardship, regulated or sensitive work, "same company, wait for the first reply", or "already in conversation".

## Banned (any one means rewrite)

- Disclaimer cheese: "not pitching you", "no pitch", "comparing notes", "picking your brain", "circling back", "reaching out".
- The announcement family: "quick question", "quick one", "curious", "just curious", "bumping this", "just following up", "just checking in", "just wanted to", "thought I'd", "hope you don't mind", "sorry to bother you". Delete the announcement, keep the sentence.
- Hollow openers: "I'd love to connect", "hope this message finds you well", "I came across your profile", "I was impressed by", "I noticed you work at".
- Signal references: "saw your post", "noticed your headline", "read your article", "your recent post".
- Vendor tells: "I help X do Y", "I build...", "check out...", any product name, any price.
- Corporate words: leverage, synergy, optimize, ROI, value proposition, solution, unlock, transform, game-changer, supercharge, seamless, robust, 10x, empower, hustle.
- Filler closers: "would love your thoughts", "how are you finding it".

## The two follow-ups (columns `DM Follow-up 1 (Day 3-4)` and `DM Follow-up 2 (Day 7-10)`)

Both only if there is still no reply, both under 300 characters, both zero pitch.

1. **Follow-up 1:** a neutral continuation. One new, small angle on the same question, or one useful observation for their case. It must make sense on its own.
2. **Follow-up 2:** a genuinely new angle, or the straight question ("Is this worth keeping open, or should I leave it?"). Then the sequence stops.
3. Never mention their silence before 5 to 7 days have passed since the last message, and never joke about it. Never two nudges in a row.

## Self-check before saving (rebuild if any box fails)

- Would a stranger read past line 1?
- Does it open from their reality, with no quote and no "your post"?
- Is the question answerable in 3 words, about them, with two easy doors?
- Zero pitch, under 300 characters, only the first name merged?
- No banned phrase, no em dash, and no two DMs in the batch open the same way?
- 8th grade or simpler, in their language?
- Could anyone else have sent this exact DM? If yes, push further.
