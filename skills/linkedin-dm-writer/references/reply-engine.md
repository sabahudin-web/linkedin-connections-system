# When they reply: turning a reply into a call

Adapted from the sell-by-chat engine in `acquisition-linkedin-dm-sell-by-chat`. Use it when a lead answers a DM or a cold email. `dm-floor.md` owns the messages nobody has answered yet; this file owns everything after the first real reply. Nothing here sends anything: write the next message, the user sends it.

## 1. Read the stage first, from the thread itself

| Stage | How you know | The job of the next message |
|---|---|---|
| Talking | A real exchange, but no problem named | Find out what their week looks like |
| Problem named | They named a bottleneck | Find out what "fixed" looks like, in their words |
| Gap open | They said the problem AND what they want instead | The only stage where asking for a call is allowed |
| Stalled | A blocker sits on the thread (price, timing, doubt) | The objection frame below, then back one stage |

No call ask before Gap open. Ask earlier and the best case is a polite yes that never turns into a booking.

## 2. Get A, then B, in their words

- **A, where they are:** ask about the mechanics of their week, never their feelings. "What does that look like when a new lead comes in?" · "Where does it usually get stuck?" · "How much of that is still you, by hand?"
- **B, where they want to be:** ask what the fix would free up. "If that ran by itself, what would you do with the time?" · "Is it the hours you want back, or the things that get dropped?"
- Let them say it. Never name their pain back bigger than they said it, never invent urgency, never put an outcome in their mouth.

## 3. The ask ladder

Each ask is answerable in about 3 words and asks a little more than the last:

1. open question: "what does that look like right now?"
2. either-or: "is it the follow-up, or the proposals?"
3. permission: "want me to send it?"
4. two named times: "Tuesday 11 or Wednesday 2, whichever is easier?" Check the user's calendar first; two real slots, never three.
5. the booking link: only when they ask for one, or after they pick a time.

If they pick a time, the whole next message is the confirmation. Creating the calendar invite waits for the user.

## 4. Objections, one message, four steps

1. Acknowledge the concern in their words.
2. Ask for the context behind it.
3. One piece of reassurance, only if it is true and comes from the user's proof list (`proof.md`).
4. One easy next step sized to where the chat is.

Use "and", not "but". Never argue. If they hold the objection after one honest pass, log it and move on.

## 5. Proof in a reply

Send proof only when it is the right next move: they named a problem, they doubt it works, or they asked for an example. Never in message 1. Pick ONE item from the user's proof list that matches how they are set up (team or solo) and the words they used, with one line on why this one. A mismatched case study is worse than none. If nothing fits, send none.

## 6. Self-check

- Do I know the stage from the thread, not a guess?
- If it asks for a call, have they said what "fixed" looks like?
- Is the ask answerable in about 3 words, and does it carry something the last message did not?
- One idea, one ask, one link at most, plain words, nothing from the banned lists.
